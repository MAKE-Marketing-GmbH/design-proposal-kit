# Style references

A direction built on taste alone is a guess. A direction built on references is
an argument. This is where the references come from and how to pick them.

## Rules

- **Style beats industry.** A moving company can be shown a Japanese furniture
  shop. What transfers is the hierarchy and the rhythm, not the trade. Looking
  only at other movers produces a site that looks like every other mover.
- **Never use competitors as the style reference.** The client's own current site
  is a source of **facts**, never of look.
- **Search wide, take few.** Two or three references per direction. Twenty
  screenshots with no decision is a moodboard, not a reference, and it tells the
  image model nothing.
- **Transfer the principle, never the surface.** Take the order of claim, proof
  and CTA. Take the spacing rhythm. Never take the colours, the fonts or the
  layout one to one.

## Do not hardcode a list of good sites

A list of reference URLs is half dead within a year: sites get redesigned,
studios close, domains lapse. Open it later and you cannot tell what it stood
for.

The curated galleries maintain their style tags permanently. **A tag is a
standing query**: it returns good examples of the same look today and in two
years. That is the library.

## The styles

Eight cover almost everything. The name doubles as the name the direction can
carry in the proposal.

| Style | How you recognise it | Fits when |
|---|---|---|
| **Swiss Rational** | Strict grid, one grotesque, no decoration, hierarchy from size and space alone | The client is precise, technical, serious |
| **Editorial Serif** | Serif headlines, magazine feel, generous white space, the text is the hero | There is something to tell |
| **Warm Craft** | Cream and earth, texture, soft edges, human rather than slick | Family business, closeness, been here a while |
| **Bright Photographic** | White, air, one accent, the photography carries everything | Real, good photos exist. Without them it collapses |
| **Dark Premium** | Dark flat field, one accent, large type | High price, exclusive, read in the evening |
| **Bold Colour Blocks** | Big colour fields, tiles, loud, memorable | The client is brave, and has no photos |
| **Brutalist Raw** | Mono type, hard edges, visible grid, deliberately unpolished | Creative, young, wants to provoke |
| **Soft Modern** | Rounded corners, soft shadows, friendly, lots of radius | Software, service, low threshold |

Three directions per proposal. **One should be the deliberate outlier**, or the
client is choosing between shades of one idea and the exercise is theatre.

## The reference card

Every reference you keep gets one. Without it, nobody later knows why it is
there, and a reference without a reason is decoration.

```
URL:
Checked:
What problem it solves:
Transferable principle:
Surface that does NOT transfer:
Licence or usage limit:
Why picked:
Why the runner-up was rejected:
```

## Where the screenshots go

Into `bundles/<slug>/references/`, and attached to the image chat alongside the
job block. A model shown an example produces something far closer than a model
told about one in words.

**They never go into the client-facing PDF.** Somebody else's website in a
commercial document you send a client is a rights problem, and the client should
be judging your design, not other people's sites.
